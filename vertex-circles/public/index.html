Vertex circles in motion

One circle through three neighbouring points measures how sharply a path bends. Here it follows an eyebrow, a runner’s hip, and every swinging limb.

κk = 2 sin φk ⁄ ‖γk+1 − γk−1‖Vertex osculating circle, Bobenko, Discrete Differential Geometry, §2.3.1

Drag the middle point γ1. A straight line gives κ = 0; a sharp corner gives a tiny circle.

Eyebrow

In the ground view, a planted foot stands still. Its neighbouring vertices land on the same spot, so no circle exists there and the table shows “planted”.

All motion here comes from idealised models: an inverted pendulum for walking, a spring and free fall for jogging and running, and smooth joint-angle patterns for the limbs. The eyebrow poses are illustrative values, not measurements. The curvature formula is exact, so the circles are true vertex osculating circles of the sampled points.
